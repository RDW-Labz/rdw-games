### A. **Cinderhold** — an isometric ARPG on Flare's painted art and DCSS's vault corpus (approved 2026-10-30)

Raised the same day as Michael's *"i want something closer to aaa"* and the clone sweep that
followed, and approved with *"both and drop title list"* — both this and Wardenmarch. Shipped as
`games/cinderhold/`; the proposal text below is kept as the record of what was approved.

**One sentence:** you descend a vault that never repeats — painted isometric rooms, loot rolled
from a table of several hundred items, a boss every five floors — and only what you carry out
survives.

#### What it's actually like to play

1. **Move and fight.** Tap to step, hold to swing, swipe to dodge, three skills on cooldowns. One
   hero, no squad menus. Everything resolves in about 40 minutes a run.
2. **The floors.** Each depth is generated by DCSS's own layout scripts — 144 `.des` files of
   room templates, corridor vaults and special set-pieces — not hand-authored maps. Same seed,
   same vault; new seed, a vault nobody has seen.
3. **The loot.** Weapons, armour, rings, potions and scrolls come out of DCSS's item tables, with
   its own enchantment and ego rules. Flare supplies the painted item art (its icon
   sheets) and the monster art that swings them.
4. **The monsters.** DCSS's monster table sets stats, rarity and depth bands; Flare's painted
   creature art (and its animation frames) set how they look and move.
5. **The pressure.** Depth is difficulty: monsters deeper down are statistically nastier, and the
   floor's own layout decides whether you can retreat. Death drops everything you carried that
   run — gold banks, gear does not.
6. **Why it reads as expensive.** Every room is painted, not drawn from primitives; monsters are
   animated 3D renders, not 16-pixel glyphs; music changes with depth; hits have weight —
   hitstop, shake, damage numbers, a sound for every interaction class.

#### Where each part comes from

| Donor (licence) | What is taken | Load-bearing at runtime? | What is *not* taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flare** (art CC-BY-SA 3.0, engine GPL-3.0) | **721 art files / 555 MB** — 93 painted enemy sheets (286 MB, bosses up to 6144×6144), 12 isometric tilesets (47 MB), 73 portraits, 103 menu panels, 5 icon sheets — plus **136 music/SFX ogg (49 MB)** and 197 item definition files; its data-driven item/stat schema as the shape of our item records | **Yes for the look** — strip the atlases and there is no game. The rules are ours. | Flare's engine and its own campaign/mod content: we reimplement, we don't run their code |
| **Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup** (tiles CC0, code GPL-2.0) | **144 `.des` layout generators**, the item/monster/spell **data tables**, and **8,796 CC0 tiles** used for UI icons and drop glyphs | **Yes** — `HYBRID_CHECKS` asserts two seeds give different floor graphs and one seed gives identical ones, and that a rolled item's stats come from the table, not a constant | Its engine, its ASCII mode, its turn-based rules (this is real-time), its 400-page spell list (we take ~30) |

Two donors, clean split: **look from Flare, generation and content tables from DCSS.** No third
clone. The same shape as the four shipped hybrids, one tier up in production value.

#### What it costs, honestly

- **Art subset:** tiles + 60 monsters + 40 items + 4 music tracks ≈ **25–35 MB** vendored
  (Flare's full 555 MB would be a first-run download, which the collection does not allow).
- **Code:** ~**4,500 lines**, the largest title in the repo. Three milestones:
  - **M1** — one hero, one floor type, DCSS generation, tap/hold combat, loot pickup. Playable.
  - **M2** — depth bands, monsters from tables, skills, boss floors, death/drop rules.
  - **M3** — the look: painted atlas swap-in, animation frames, music by depth, hit feedback,
    town meta, then touch/offline/long-run passes.


### B. **Wardenmarch** — a skirmish campaign on Wesnoth's unit rules over DCSS arenas (approved 2026-10-30)

The alternative that was offered alongside Cinderhold — squads instead of loot — and approved in
the same breath. Shipped as `games/wardenmarch/`.

**One sentence:** you field four soldiers whose every stat is Battle for Wesnoth's own, and fight
them over a battlefield cut from a Dungeon Crawl vault, where doors and pillars decide what your
archers can actually see.

- **Systems:** hp, movement allowance, recruit cost, alignment, and each attack's
  damage/number/range read out of 22 Wesnoth unit definitions (327 unit `.cfg` files exist in the
  donor; 22 are vendored with their sprites and portraits). Melee cannot reach two tiles, a
  5-move unit has 5 points — the rules are upstream, not invented.
- **Arenas:** the same DCSS corpus Cinderhold uses, filtered to 10–22-wide vaults. 14 Wesnoth
  terrain tiles are placed as the ground, so the board reads as Wesnoth even though its shape is
  DCSS's.
- **Loop:** pick a squad, take a turn each unit (move, then attack), hand over to a readable AI,
  repeat until one side holds the field. Campaign progress banks between battles.

## Awaiting opt-in

### 1. **Demurrage** — a freight company on Endless Sky's real galaxy (proposed 2026-10-30)

Raised after Michael's note that the shipped eight read as small games. Nothing here is built;
this ships only if he opts in, per `docs/SCOPE-GATE.md`.

**One sentence:** you run a licensed freight line across Endless Sky's actual 685-system galaxy —
buying hulls, writing timetables, feeding your own refineries, and losing money every hour a ship
sits idle.

#### What it's actually like to play

A phone-shaped management game, not a twitch game. The screen is a star map you can pinch and pan,
a list of ships, a contract board, and — on any port — a timetable editor. One in-game hour per
tick; you pause, plan, then fast-forward.

1. **The board.** Ports post contracts: *"90 units of Food, Tais → Ascella, in 40 hours,
   12,400 cr, −900/hr demurrage after that."* The prices behind those contracts are not invented —
   they are Endless Sky's own per-system trade table (that pair really is 340 → 467 cr in the data).
   The visual mock is `docs/mocks/demurrage.html`.
2. **The timetable.** You give each hull an **order list**, the way OpenTTD gives a bus one:
   a loop of stops, and per stop a rule — *buy here*, *sell there*, *wait for full load*,
   *skip if empty*, *repeat 3×*. Set an offset so three hulls don't all hit the same berth.
   The ship then runs itself, hour by hour, through real jump distances taken from system
   coordinates.
3. **The margin.** A jump costs fuel by distance and hull mass; the sale price is the destination
   port's real price. Profit is the spread between two ports minus fuel minus demurrage. Endless
   Sky's spread runs 103 to 1,508 credits a unit, so a good route and a bad route are not close.
4. **The works.** Any port with an ore deposit can take a refinery; a refinery eats ore and fuel
   per tick and produces Metal or Equipment into port stock. You then have to *haul your own
   inputs* — the ship that feeds the refinery is the ship not earning on the board. A starved
   works idles and its port rating falls.
5. **The rating.** Every port scores you 0–100 for service: deliveries in the last N hours, how
   long its cargo waited, how often you skipped it. Rating buys better contracts and cheaper fuel;
   neglect decays it. This is the pressure that stops "one perfect route" from being the whole game.
6. **The risk.** Lanes carry a piracy weight taken from ES's fleet spawn tables. Unescorted
   high-value convoys get raided; raids resolve instantly against hull/shield stats from ES's
   120-hull table (the same mapping Ember Drift already uses), costing cargo, hull points and
   hours. You either pay insurance or buy an escort hull and eat its upkeep.
7. **The clock.** A rival line bids on the contracts you skip, so waiting is a decision too.
8. **The run.** Pay off the lease, own five hulls, clear the charter: 60–90 minutes for someone
   who plans well. A company profile persists between runs (hull classes unlocked, sector seed),
   so ten hours in is a different game than hour one.

#### Where each part comes from

| Donor (licence) | What is taken | Load-bearing at runtime? | What is *not* taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Endless Sky** (GPL-3.0) | `data/map systems.txt` → **685 systems** with real coordinates and **1,612 lane links** as the map graph; **4,800 real `trade` price rows** (10 commodities, 103–1,508 cr) as the entire market; **1,146 `minables` rows** as ore deposits that decide where a refinery can go; **3,102 `fleet` rows** as lane-risk weights; `data/map planets.txt` + per-region planet files → **619 planets, 369 with a spaceport**, as the ports; the 120-hull stat table and the **118 ship sprites** already vendored under `vendor/endless-sky/` (13 of them are what Ember Drift flies). | **Yes** — the world, the prices and the hulls are ES's data. Break the table and there is no economy. | Its engine, flight physics, mission/quest scripting, star and nebula imagery, the other ~107 hull sprites (authored silhouettes instead), its sound. |
| **OpenTTD** (GPL-2.0) | The **systems**: vehicle order lists with per-stop load rules and repeat loops, timetable offsets, the station-rating model (cargo waiting vs service frequency, decaying), and town/industry growth driven by service quality. Its competitor model (a rival that takes the work you decline) is the basis of the rival line. | **Yes** — the timetable engine *is* the game; `HYBRID_CHECKS` asserts a route runs unattended and that rating moves with service and decays without it. | Its sprite sets (8bpp DOS-palette GRFs that would need a decoder and would clash with ES sprites), its tile map, its pathfinding, its code. Systems are reimplemented, not copied. |
| **Mindustry** (GPL-3.0) | The **production model** — ore → alloy → component chains with per-tick throughput and input starvation — plus the 35 already-vendored sprites (conveyors, drill, turret, item icons for copper/carbide/phase-fabric…) for the works UI and building icons. | **Yes** — the upgrade axis; `HYBRID_CHECKS` starves a refinery and requires it to idle at zero output, then resume when a haul lands. | Its ECS engine and simulation code (logic written from scratch, the same rule Siegehaus follows), its maps, its units. |

Mechanics come from OpenTTD and Mindustry; *bytes* come from Endless Sky and Mindustry. That is
the same split the four shipped hybrids use, and it keeps the licence story simple: GPL data and
sprites vendored with attribution, two rule systems reimplemented.

#### Why this is a bigger game than the eight we have

| | Current titles | Demurrage |
|---|---|---|
| Session | 3–10 minute arcade run | 60–90 minute company run, then a persistent profile |
| Content | 9 rooms / one island / one sector | 685 systems, 369 ports, 4,800 price points |
| Systems that interact | 2 | 5 (timetable, market, works, rating, risk) with the same clock |
| Failure mode | you die | you go broke slowly, which is a more interesting failure |

Effort, honestly: the largest title in the repo — about **4,500–5,500 lines** against 1,680–3,273
for the current eight, in three buildable milestones:

- **M1 — the line runs** (ES map + hulls + market + contracts + OpenTTD timetables). Playable,
  and the first two systems are load-bearing.
- **M2 — the works** (Mindustry chains, ore deposits, starvation, port rating consequences).
- **M3 — pressure and balance** (piracy/escorts, rival line, debt, rating decay, save/meta,
  HYBRID_CHECKS, offline + touch + long-run passes).

**Decision needed:** opt in, or not. Also worth naming: whether the title stays **Demurrage**
(alternatives: *Wayfreight*, *Tenderhold*), whether piracy resolves automatically or as a short
manual fight, and whether the rival line is in M1 or M3.

### 3. The port builds are weaker on a phone than their score suggests

Stated plainly rather than hidden in the catalog:

- **Luanti** — the built APK in the reference tree is `arm64-v8a` only. Fine on any modern
  phone; it will not install on an old 32-bit device. Rebuilding all four ABIs is a Gradle run
  with NDK 28.2.
- **Endless Sky** — no Android APK exists in the clone. The verified artifact is the desktop
  binary. On a phone this is a "run it on a desktop host" title.
- **DDNet** — the APK exists, but the game's best mode is public servers. Practice and local
  servers are offline; the headline experience is not. It is the one port that stretches the
  "offline by construction" rule.

**Decision needed:** keep all three with the caveats recorded (current state), or swap Endless
Sky / DDNet for APK-backed offline titles from §1?

### 4. Not absorbed: anything from the legacy business systems

No CRM, invoicing, marketplace, or messaging code from any other project was read into this
repo. The collection is standalone. If you want the shell's save-export to feed a real backend,
that is a new decision, not a default.
